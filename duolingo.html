01 / Overview

You can understand the words, but still miss the conversation.

As international students navigating life in the U.S., my teammates and I experienced firsthand how knowing English doesn't always mean understanding everyday conversations.

From slang to humor and unspoken social norms, communication often requires cultural knowledge that traditional language lessons don't cover.

02 / The Problem

Language proficiency doesn't always translate into social confidence.

While language-learning apps help users build vocabulary and grammar skills, they often provide limited opportunities to practice informal, culturally nuanced conversations.

For newcomers, this gap can make everyday social interactions confusing and intimidating.

✳ A LITTLE STORY

Fluent on paper. Lost in conversation.

What happens when knowing English isn't quite enough?

THE DESIGN CHALLENGE

How might we help language learners feel more confident navigating everyday conversations and cultural differences?

03 / Research & Key Insights

Understanding What Learners Actually Struggle With

We started with a simple assumption: newcomers needed more knowledge about American culture.

But after speaking with four international students and newcomers, we began to see a different problem. It wasn't always about knowing more. Sometimes, it was about understanding what people actually meant in everyday conversations.

01 /

Knowing the language doesn't always mean knowing what to say.

Participants described difficulties with slang, abbreviations, and small talk, even when they understood the individual words.

This shifted our focus from teaching general cultural knowledge to helping users practice everyday conversations.

02 /

A feature can be useful, but still difficult to discover.

During usability testing, participants initially interpreted our Culture Hub icon as a friends feature.

We realized that users needed to understand what a feature was before deciding to explore it.

03 /

Learning with others should feel encouraging, not limiting.

Participants pointed out that their friends might not always be available to join daily challenges.

This led us to offer the flexibility to compete with friends or strangers.

04 / Design Exploration & Iterations

Small Changes, Meaningful Differences

Once we had a clearer direction, we began exploring how cultural learning could fit naturally into Duolingo's existing experience.

Through usability testing with four participants, we discovered that some of our initial design choices weren't as intuitive as we had expected.

EXPLORING POSSIBILITIES

Where Could Cultural Learning Fit?

We explored three ways cultural learning could become part of the Duolingo experience, each addressing a different moment in the learning journey.

01 /

Integrated Learning

Introducing cultural context within existing lessons, so learners could encounter everyday expressions as they learn.

02 /

Culture Hub

Creating a dedicated space where learners could explore cultural topics and practice conversations beyond regular lessons.

03 /

Daily Challenge

Making cultural practice more engaging through short challenges and opportunities to learn alongside others.

Rather than choosing just one direction, our final concept brought elements of these approaches together.

DECISION 01

Making Culture Learning Easier to Find

We tested two entry points for the Culture Hub: a dedicated navigation icon and an option within the More menu.

Participants generally preferred the dedicated entry point, but the original icon was mistaken for a friends feature.

01A / Comparing Entry Points

01B / Refining the Icon

We kept the dedicated entry point and replaced the icon with a globe to better communicate its purpose.

DECISION 02

Making Challenges More Flexible

We initially designed daily challenges around competing with friends.

However, participants pointed out that friends might not always be available when they wanted to play.

We introduced separate options to compete with friends or strangers, giving learners more freedom to participate on their own time.

DECISION 03

Making Achievements Worth Sharing

During usability testing, three out of four participants suggested adding a way to share their achievements.

This made us reconsider how the leaderboard could support social engagement beyond competition itself.

We added a sharing feature and clarified the leaderboard's name to connect it more closely to cultural challenges.

AFTER
DECISION 04

Balancing Playfulness and Readability

During usability testing, one participant pointed out that the text on the Culture Hub was difficult to read against its brightly colored backgrounds.

While we wanted the interface to reflect Duolingo's playful personality, this feedback made us reconsider how our color choices affected readability.

We softened the card backgrounds and strengthened text contrast to make the content easier to read while preserving the playful visual style.

05 / Introducing the Solution

Bringing Culture Into Everyday Learning

We wanted cultural learning to feel like a natural part of Duolingo, rather than a separate experience.

Our final concept brings together three features that help learners discover cultural knowledge, practice real-world conversations, and stay engaged.

01 / CULTURE HUB

A Space to Explore Beyond Vocabulary

We introduced a dedicated Culture Hub where learners can explore everyday expressions, social norms, and cultural topics.

By integrating it into Duolingo's navigation, we wanted to make cultural learning easy to discover and return to.

02 / CONVERSATION EXERCISES

Practicing What People Actually Say

Understanding an expression is one thing. Knowing when and how to use it is another.

We designed scenario-based exercises that introduce slang, idioms, and everyday conversations in context.

Instead of simply memorizing phrases, learners can explore how language changes across different social situations.

03 / DAILY CHALLENGES

Learning Together, at Your Own Pace

We explored how social challenges could make cultural learning more engaging.

Learners can participate in challenges with friends or other users, without having to rely on their friends' availability.

We also introduced a way to share achievements, creating opportunities for connection beyond the challenge itself.

06 / Reflection

What I Learned Along the Way

01 /

Challenging Our Own Assumptions

One of our biggest challenges was recognizing how much our own experiences influenced our initial ideas.

We started by assuming that learners needed more cultural information. But through research, we realized that their everyday struggles were often much more specific, like understanding slang or knowing how to respond in casual conversations.

This reminded me that even when I can personally relate to a problem, I shouldn't assume everyone experiences it the same way.

02 /

Learning to Listen Without Leading

During user interviews, I realized how easy it is to unintentionally guide someone's answer through the way I phrase a question.

I also learned that meaningful improvements don't always require big redesigns. Sometimes, something as small as changing an icon can make a feature easier to understand.

03 /

What I Would Explore Next

With more time, I would test our designs with a larger and more diverse group of language learners, especially people with different cultural backgrounds and levels of language proficiency.

I'd also love to explore how this concept could extend beyond American culture, making cultural learning more relevant to people learning different languages around the world.
